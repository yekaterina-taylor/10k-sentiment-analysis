How to read this: what each sentiment measure means and how to read the colors

These measures come from the Loughran-McDonald dictionary, word lists built specifically for financial documents. The program counts how many words from each list appear in the MD&A. A word can be on more than one list (for example, may counts as both uncertain and weak modal). The bar chart shows each count per 1,000 words, so a longer MD&A does not automatically score higher.

Most words in a 10-K are neutral (revenue, fiscal, product), so only about 2–3% of words carry sentiment. That is why sentiment scores are small numbers close to zero.

How to read the correlations

  • Correlation runs from −1 to +1. Near 0 means no relationship; near +1 means the two move together.
  • With only ten years (nine year-to-year changes), a correlation needs to be about ±0.67 or larger before it is statistically meaningful. Smaller values can easily happen by chance.

Conclusion

What the side-by-side comparison shows.

Key findings

  1. Size vs. growth. Apple is the larger company by revenue ($416B vs. $216B in the latest year), but NVIDIA grew far faster: revenue rose about 31× (47% a year) versus about 1.9× (8% a year) for Apple. In the latest year NVIDIA's net income ($120B) passed Apple's ($112B) on about half the revenue.
  2. Profitability. Both are very profitable. Apple's net margin is steady at 21–27%; NVIDIA's is higher but more volatile, from 16% in FY2023 to about 56% in FY2025–FY2026.
  3. Liquidity and solvency show two different strategies. NVIDIA holds far more current assets than current liabilities (current ratio 3.5–8.0) and modest debt (debt-to-assets 0.24–0.46). Apple has run a current ratio below 1.0 since FY2022 and debt-to-assets around 0.8, because it returns cash through large share buybacks. For a company with Apple's cash flow this is a choice, not a sign of distress.
  4. Efficiency. Both now produce more than $1 of sales per $1 of assets. Apple got there in FY2021; NVIDIA in FY2024, when AI demand pushed revenue up much faster than assets.
  5. Tone. Despite very different growth, the two MD&As sound similar: both are net negative in almost every year (average P/N about 0.65 for Apple, 0.59 for NVIDIA). Apple's MD&A uses more uncertainty and legal language per 1,000 words; NVIDIA's is longer and discusses supply, export controls, and acquisitions.
  6. Price vs. sentiment. For neither company does MD&A tone track or predict the stock price in a statistically meaningful way. NVIDIA's stock rose about 67× and Apple's about 9×, following earnings growth rather than the wording of the annual report. MD&A tone mostly reflects the risks and events management must disclose.

Limitations

  • Only ten annual observations per company, so correlations are not statistically reliable.
  • Dictionary methods count words without context: "critical" in the heading "Critical Accounting Estimates" counts as negative, and acquisition terms such as "termination" or "closing" can be miscounted.
  • Fiscal years are aligned by calendar overlap; the two companies' year-ends are about four months apart.
